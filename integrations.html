01
Four ways in

In this order of preference, and never the other way round.

The route is chosen by what the system actually supports, not by what is quickest for us. A system with no modern API is a normal Tuesday, not a reason to say no.

1 API

Official, documented, versioned. Where it exists this is always the answer.

2 Database

Read directly, write through a controlled layer with the vendor's blessing where the vendor still exists.

3 Files

Scheduled exports and imports. Unglamorous, entirely reliable, and often what a twenty-year-old system was designed for.

4 The interface itself

UI automation, when there is genuinely nothing else. It is the last resort and we will say so out loud rather than quietly billing for it.

04
Three constants

The same three rules on every integration we build.

A human approves anything sensitive.

Defined per step, named in the design document, and enforced in the code rather than in a policy nobody reads.

Everything lands in an audit trail.

Who or what did it, when, on which record, and what the previous value was. Written so an auditor can follow it without us in the room.

Roles and monitoring on day one.

Not retrofitted after go-live, when it is both more expensive and less complete.

European clients Hosting and data residency follow your architecture.

Your own tenant, your own servers, or an EU cloud region. Hosting regions, subprocessors, retention and access controls are agreed before implementation and documented in the architecture and DPA. We design solutions to support your GDPR and EU AI Act requirements.

US partners We start with a conversation, not a data-centre map.

With American partners the first work is consulting: understanding what you already run, where your own delivery is stretched, and where we can genuinely help. Hosting follows from that decision rather than leading it.
